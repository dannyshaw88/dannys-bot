# Ban Dataset Interpretation Rules

## Rule 1 — Timestamp clustering is NOT a server sweep
Accounts marked banned at the same second or within seconds of each other are that way because Danny manually batch-marks them. There is no such thing as an "IP sweep" or "coordinated server action" in this data. Never use timestamp proximity as evidence of server-side batch action.

## Rule 2 — "SLOW" trustScore is a user-assigned label
The "SLOW" badge visible in the trustScore/note field on an account is NOT auto-generated by the app. It was manually assigned by Danny in a previous session note in account settings. It reflects his subjective assessment from an earlier run, not any computed metric from the current session.

## Rule 3 — Re-added timestamp: LESS suspicion, not more
If an account has a `re-added` timestamp in account settings, that account was previously active, removed (possibly due to a lock, NOT necessarily a ban — a 3-day lock can cause removal and re-adding), and then re-added. Re-added accounts actually have LESS suspicion than fresh/new accounts because they have already passed Instagram's initial new-account scrutiny. Fresh first-time accounts face the MOST scrutiny. A re-add could be from: account lock (3-day temporary lock), password reset, or operator preference — do NOT assume ban history. The re-add timestamp is context for understanding account lifecycle, not a risk amplifier.

## Rule 4 — followCountBeforeBan is unreliable
Most accounts show 0 in `followCountBeforeBan` due to a tracking issue. Use raw endpoint snapshots to count actual follows — do not trust this field for analysis.

## Rule 5 — Survivors not in the export
The 2 survivor accounts are still live. They are NOT in the export file. They were counted in the summary only.
